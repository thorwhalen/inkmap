inkmap.locate

inkmap.locate¶

Locators: finding where a model sits in an image.

A locator is a plain function (bgr, gray) -> list[Placement], so registering your own needs no subclassing:

from inkmap import locate

locate.LOCATORS[“mine”] = my_locator inkmap.find_all(photo, locators=[“mine”, “qr_zxing”])

For the many cases where the mark cannot be auto-detected – a letter, a logo, a shape with no decoder in the world – place() is the universal entry point: you supply the model and where its corners are, and the rest of the package proceeds identically. That is the seam that makes this work for “anything the user provides a model for”.

Plain registration marks – solid squares and nested-square bullseyes, which carry no code to decode – are found by find_square_marks(), which returns their projective sub-pixel centres rather than placements of a model. A plain printed patch – a uniform colour rectangle, no code – and the ring of substrate round it are found by locate_patch(), seeded at a point inside it.

Bases: object

A located patch: masks over the frame, its geometry, and how far to trust them.

patch is the interior, eroded clear of the edge; substrate a ring of bare substrate just outside; region the whole patch, uneroded. quad is the patch’s corners, clockwise as seen on screen from the one nearest the image’s top-left, to a fraction of a pixel when its sides could be fitted (None if no region was found). ok is not notes.

The rest say why: seed_lab the patch colour, threshold_delta_e the split used, area_share of the frame, rectangularity (region over fitted quad), outline_deviation_px (95th percentile of the outline’s distance from the fitted sides), hole_share (share of the interior not the patch’s colour), substrate_px, and ring_px (the ring’s inner and outer distance).

property placement: Placement | None¶

The patch and its substrate ring as a placement, or None without a quad.

The model is the patch as foreground inside a border of background as wide as the substrate ring’s outer edge, in units of about one pixel. So inkmap.analyze() and inkmap.measure() treat the patch as any other mark, and measure compares the patch with the substrate round it. The placement’s quad is that bordered model’s, not the patch’s: the patch’s own corners are quad. measure averages the whole border, background and all, where substrate keeps only its paper-like pixels: on a thin paper border, measure with substrate instead.

One square mark found in an image. Coordinates are pixels, (x, y).

corners are the outer square’s, clockwise as seen on screen, starting from the one nearest the image’s top-left. A square has no intrinsic first corner, so that order is a convention, not an identification. rings is the side of each nested square as a fraction of the outer one, outermost first (empty for a solid mark). centres are the outer square’s centre followed by each nested square’s, each where that square’s diagonals cross; centre is their mean weighted by side, which for a solid mark is the outer square’s own.

inkmap.locate.find_square_marks(gray, *, min_side_px=8.0, max_side_share=0.1, polarity=Polarity.FOREGROUND_IS_DARK, dark_share=0.72, min_contrast=6.0, local_window_share=0.12)[source]¶

Every solid or nested square mark in gray (2-D, 0-255 scale), largest first.

A mark’s outer square must be at least min_side_px on a side and at most max_side_share of the frame’s shorter side. Nested marks report their ring ratios; solid marks report none. polarity says whether the marks read darker than the substrate (the usual case) or brighter.

The foreground is binarised relative to the local mean brightness: a pixel is foreground when it is darker than dark_share of the mean over a window local_window_share of the frame’s shorter side, and darker by at least min_contrast grey levels. The window is widened to stay wider than the largest mark looked for, since a mark wider than it is hollowed by its own ink.

Anything square is a candidate: a QR symbol’s clusters of data modules and some printed glyphs come back as solid marks too. Telling the registration marks from them, by size, ring ratio and layout, is the caller’s.

inkmap.locate.locate(bgr, gray, *, locators=('qr_zxing', 'qr_aruco', 'qr_opencv', 'barcode_zxing'), first_hit=False, scale=None, full_res_retry=True)[source]¶

Run locator strategies and merge their results, de-duplicating by position.

Strategies are tried in order. With first_hit=True the cascade stops at the first strategy that finds anything – cheaper, and usually right when you know there is one mark. Otherwise every strategy runs and results merge, which is what earns the recall when an image holds several marks that no single detector finds all of.

Coarse-to-fine. scale (0 < scale <= 1) runs the cascade on a copy of the frame downscaled by that factor (cv2.INTER_AREA), then maps every placement back to full-resolution pixels. Locating costs roughly in proportion to pixel count, so on a 4000 x 3000 photograph 0.5 is about 4x faster and 0.35 about 7x. None (the default) or 1 locate at full resolution, exactly as without the argument.

Every locator – a registered name or your own callable – is handed the downscaled bgr and gray (and, on a retry, a full-resolution crop), and must return placements in the coordinates of the frame it was given; mapping them back to the full frame is done here. A locator that bakes in absolute pixel sizes or positions will misbehave under scale.

The mapping is one affine A (small -> full pixels), in the pixel-centre convention cv2.resize uses, x_full = (x_small + 0.5) / scale - 0.5, applied to both quad and transform (A @ transform). Locator name and payload are kept; metadata["locate_scale"] records the scale used. Corner conventions differ by locator (OpenCV’s are inclusive pixel indices, zxing’s are the continuous outer boundary), so a locator’s own half-pixel bias is magnified by 1 / scale on the way up – a pixel or so at 0.5. That does not matter downstream when inkmap.analyze() refines (its default), which pins the transform to sub-pixel accuracy at full resolution anyway.

The model is the fragile part, not the geometry. Locators read the model off the frame they are given – a QR’s module count and module matrix, a barcode’s bar pattern – and refinement cannot repair a wrong model, it only fits its geometry. Below about config.MIN_COARSE_PIXELS_PER_FEATURE (4) pixels per module in the downscaled frame those reads go wrong while the code still decodes: a 25 x 25 QR read as 21 x 21, or tens of flipped modules. So, with full_res_retry (default on; only meaningful with scale < 1), a coarse placement is re-located on a padded full-resolution crop around it when either its payload is None (found but not decoded – typically a small, far-away code) or its pixels per feature in the downscaled frame are under that floor. The full-resolution result replaces it (metadata["locate_retry"] names the reason, "undecoded" or "undersampled", and locate_scale becomes 1.0). Crops are small, so this costs little; pick scale so your codes clear the floor (at 0.5, 8 px per module at full resolution) and it never triggers. A coarse placement under the floor that is kept anyway – full_res_retry=False, or the crop found nothing – carries metadata["locate_undersampled"] = True: treat its model as unreliable. A mark the coarse pass misses entirely is not recovered: lower the downscaling instead.

Return type:

list[Placement]

inkmap.locate.locate_patch(image, *, seed='centre', seed_share=0.04, min_delta_e=4.0, max_delta_e=25.0, edge_margin_share=0.08, ring_share=(0.02, 0.06), min_area_share=0.01, min_rectangularity=0.85, min_substrate_px=50, min_substrate_lift=1.0, max_substrate_chroma=15.0, min_side_share=0.15, min_edge_step=3.0)[source]¶

The uniform patch at seed, and the ring of substrate just outside it.

image is a path, a BGR (or BGRA) array, or a grey array, 8-bit. seed is "centre" or an (x, y) pixel inside the patch; its colour is the median of a window seed_share of the frame’s shorter side across. Pixels within the Otsu split of the CIELAB distance to it, clamped to [min_delta_e, max_delta_e], are candidates, and the connected candidate region at the seed is the patch.

edge_margin_share erodes the interior, and ring_share places the substrate ring, both as shares of the patch’s shorter side. The verdict fails, with a note saying why, when the patch is under min_area_share of the frame, less rectangular than min_rectangularity (area over its minimum-area rectangle’s: where a pale patch leaked into the paper), cut by the frame’s edge, or when the ring holds under min_substrate_px pixels of substrate, substrate no lighter than the patch by min_substrate_lift L*, or substrate on fewer than three sides (min_side_share of that side’s ring each), or when a side of the patch has no colour step of min_edge_step across it – the sign of a region that leaked into shaded paper. A failed verdict on a leaked region is retried at a tighter threshold.

inkmap.locate.place(model, quad, *, inclusive_pixel_corners=False, locator='manual', **metadata)[source]¶

Place a model at a known quadrilateral – the universal escape hatch.

quad is four (x, y) image points in the model’s own corner order: the point corresponding to model-space (0, 0) first, then clockwise. Order matters and is not inferred: sorting corners by image position silently mislabels them once the mark is rotated past 45 degrees.

Use this for anything without a decoder – printed letters, logos, engraved marks – or when you already know where the mark is and only want the coverage machinery.
