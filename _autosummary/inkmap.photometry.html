inkmap.photometry¶

Turning masks into measurements that are actually valid.

Everything here exists because a per-pixel mask is necessary but nowhere near sufficient for measuring anything about the mark – its reflectance, its density, its colour, how much of it actually printed. The failure modes are silent: they do not raise, they return a plausible number wrong by tens of percent.

Two lessons. Geometric purity is necessary but not sufficient – a pixel entirely under foreground still reads 30% low, because the point spread function drags background signal into it, and that contamination has a different footprint from partial area coverage. Only spatial erosion removes it, which is what the weight channel is.

And erosion stops working once the blur is wide relative to a model unit: there are no uncontaminated pixels left. That is the classic partial-volume result, and it is why measurability() exists – better to say the measurement is not possible than to return a confidently wrong number.

inkmap.photometry.illumination_field(image, ink_map, *, degree=1)[source]¶

Fit a smooth illumination field across the mark from its background pixels.

The background within the mark samples the illumination across the whole mark, so the scene calibrates itself – no flat-field frame required. A first-order (planar) fit handles the usual oblique-lighting gradient; degree 2 handles vignetting curvature at the cost of being able to absorb real signal.

Returns a field normalised to mean 1.0 over the mark; divide by it to flatten.

Return type:

ndarray

inkmap.photometry.measure(image, ink_map, *, linearize=True, flatten=False, margin=None, robust=True)[source]¶

Aggregate image over the safe foreground and background pixels of ink_map.

image need not be the one the mark was located in: any frame registered to it works, which is the usual way to measure a different exposure, channel or modality using geometry established once on the sharpest frame.

linearize undoes sRGB encoding first – leave it on for anything from a normal camera pipeline, off for already-linear data. flatten divides out a fitted illumination field. robust reports medians alongside means; on real photographs they diverge exactly where something is wrong, which makes the pair a free diagnostic.

Return type:

Measurement

inkmap.photometry.measurement_mask(ink_map, *, kind='foreground', purity=0.999, margin=None)[source]¶

Pixels safe to measure: geometrically pure, then eroded clear of edges.

kind selects "foreground" or "background". purity is the coverage threshold for “pure”; the default demands essentially total coverage, which is the point – a partially covered pixel biases the mean toward the other class.

This is the mask to use for a measurement. InkMap.foreground_mask() is for display and for anything that only needs a label.

Return type:

ndarray

inkmap.photometry.srgb_to_linear(image)[source]¶

Undo the sRGB transfer function, returning float in [0, 1].

Averaging pixel values without this is a real error, not a nicety: sRGB values are perceptually encoded, so the mean of encoded values is not the encoded mean intensity. Any difference between two exposures compounds it. Apply before any arithmetic that claims to be about light.

The piecewise IEC 61966-2-1 curve, not the 2.2 power approximation.

Return type:

ndarray
