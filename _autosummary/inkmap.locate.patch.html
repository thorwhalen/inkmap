inkmap.locate.patch

inkmap.locate.patch¶

Locate a plain printed patch – a uniform colour rectangle – and the substrate round it.

The other locators start from something with a decoder or from a quad you supply. A single printed colour patch on a paper strip has neither: no code, no registration marks, an arbitrary background. To measure the patch against its own paper, a caller needs three things, and locate_patch() returns them:

import inkmap

regions = inkmap.locate_patch(photo)            # seeded at the frame's centre
regions = inkmap.locate_patch(photo, seed=(x, y))
regions.patch, regions.substrate, regions.ok, regions.notes

  • patch: the patch’s interior, eroded clear of its blurred edge;

  • substrate: a narrow ring of bare substrate just outside it;

  • ok and notes: whether to trust them, and if not, why.

regions.quad is the patch’s four corners to a fraction of a pixel, and regions.placement the same as a Placement, so the rest of inkmap (analyze, measure) works on a patch unchanged.

How. The patch colour is the median CIELAB colour of a small window round the seed. Every pixel’s distance to it is split by Otsu’s threshold (clamped to a Delta E range), and the connected region holding the seed window is kept, closed and filled. Its sides are then fitted to the colour edge – the mid-level crossing of that Delta E image – so a pale patch on white paper, whose lightness barely differs from the paper’s, is placed as precisely as a dark one.

Substrate. The ring lies between two distances from the patch, scaled to the patch’s own size, and keeps only its light, low-chroma pixels: a thin paper border is easily overshot onto the background, which is why the ring is narrow by default. The paper must also be lighter than the patch, and be found on at least three of its four sides.

The verdict fails, with a note saying why, on a patch that is too small, cut by the frame, without substrate on three sides, or that is not the shape and colour of a printed patch: an outline straying from the fitted sides (a leak, a rounded corner), no colour step across a side (a leak into shaded paper), two colours in the region or a large blob of another one inside it (a seed near the patch’s edge or on the paper). On a failed verdict the region is split again at a tighter threshold, which is how a pale patch on white paper is found at all; if no split passes, the first answer is returned with the last split’s reason appended. Text, specks or glare on the patch are left out of PatchRegions.patch; ring pixels not of the paper’s colour (a neighbouring light patch) are left out of PatchRegions.substrate.

Limits. The patch colour is one global seed colour, so strong uneven light defeats a pale patch: with lightness falling 40% across the frame, a patch a Delta E of ~10 from its paper is mostly refused (correctly, but refused). Coloured paper is refused too. Corners are good to about 0.3 px at blur up to 1.5 px, pale patches included (python scripts/validate.py patch). A 4000 x 3000 frame takes about 1.5 s.

On flash bursts, seed on an unlit frame (or the median of the unlit frames): under flash a pale patch clips to the paper’s white and the region leaks.

A located patch: masks over the frame, its geometry, and how far to trust them.

patch is the interior, eroded clear of the edge; substrate a ring of bare substrate just outside; region the whole patch, uneroded. quad is the patch’s corners, clockwise as seen on screen from the one nearest the image’s top-left, to a fraction of a pixel when its sides could be fitted (None if no region was found). ok is not notes.

The rest say why: seed_lab the patch colour, threshold_delta_e the split used, area_share of the frame, rectangularity (region over fitted quad), outline_deviation_px (95th percentile of the outline’s distance from the fitted sides), hole_share (share of the interior not the patch’s colour), substrate_px, and ring_px (the ring’s inner and outer distance).

property placement: Placement | None¶

The patch and its substrate ring as a placement, or None without a quad.

The model is the patch as foreground inside a border of background as wide as the substrate ring’s outer edge, in units of about one pixel. So inkmap.analyze() and inkmap.measure() treat the patch as any other mark, and measure compares the patch with the substrate round it. The placement’s quad is that bordered model’s, not the patch’s: the patch’s own corners are quad. measure averages the whole border, background and all, where substrate keeps only its paper-like pixels: on a thin paper border, measure with substrate instead.

inkmap.locate.patch.locate_patch(image, *, seed='centre', seed_share=0.04, min_delta_e=4.0, max_delta_e=25.0, edge_margin_share=0.08, ring_share=(0.02, 0.06), min_area_share=0.01, min_rectangularity=0.85, min_substrate_px=50, min_substrate_lift=1.0, max_substrate_chroma=15.0, min_side_share=0.15, min_edge_step=3.0)[source]¶

The uniform patch at seed, and the ring of substrate just outside it.

image is a path, a BGR (or BGRA) array, or a grey array, 8-bit. seed is "centre" or an (x, y) pixel inside the patch; its colour is the median of a window seed_share of the frame’s shorter side across. Pixels within the Otsu split of the CIELAB distance to it, clamped to [min_delta_e, max_delta_e], are candidates, and the connected candidate region at the seed is the patch.

edge_margin_share erodes the interior, and ring_share places the substrate ring, both as shares of the patch’s shorter side. The verdict fails, with a note saying why, when the patch is under min_area_share of the frame, less rectangular than min_rectangularity (area over its minimum-area rectangle’s: where a pale patch leaked into the paper), cut by the frame’s edge, or when the ring holds under min_substrate_px pixels of substrate, substrate no lighter than the patch by min_substrate_lift L*, or substrate on fewer than three sides (min_side_share of that side’s ring each), or when a side of the patch has no colour step of min_edge_step across it – the sign of a region that leaked into shaded paper. A failed verdict on a leaked region is retried at a tighter threshold.

Return type:

PatchRegions
