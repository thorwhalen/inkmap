inkmap.synth

inkmap.synth¶

Synthetic scenes with exact ground truth.

Real photographs cannot validate a coverage map. Nobody can hand-label what fraction of a pixel is foreground, and no public dataset carries per-pixel coverage ground truth for printed marks – the document-binarisation benchmarks (DIBCO) are binary. So the only way to measure whether a 0..1 output is right is to render scenes whose true coverage is known analytically.

The trick that makes it exact: render the image from the coverage map. Supersample the model through a known transform to get true coverage, then form the clean image as 255 * (1 - coverage). Ground truth and stimulus are the same object by construction, and any degradation applied afterwards (blur, noise, gradient, glare, JPEG) leaves the ground truth correct.

Works for any model, so a text or logo pipeline is validated the same way as a QR one. Real photographs remain essential for detection rate – see inkmap.corpus.

inkmap.synth.mark_sheet()[source]¶

A card of square registration marks, and where each one’s centre truly is.

Three bullseyes and a solid square at the corners, as on an OMR form, and two QR finder patterns inside: 700 x 500 cells, marks 50 cells (42 for a finder). Render it with render() at about 1.2 pixels per cell for ~60 px marks. Returns (model, truth), truth being (centre_uv, rings) per mark in model units, so cv2.perspectiveTransform through a scene’s transform gives the true image centres.

Return type:

tuple[MatrixModel, list[tuple[tuple[float, float], tuple]]]

inkmap.synth.nested_square(side, rings=())[source]¶

A square mark as a (side, side) cell grid: foreground, then alternating rings.

rings are the sides of the nested squares as fractions of side, outermost first, alternating background and foreground. Each must leave an equal whole number of cells on both sides, or the mark would be off-centre – that is an error.

>>> nested_square(7, (5 / 7, 3 / 7)).astype(int)[3]
array([1, 0, 1, 1, 1, 0, 1])

inkmap.synth.render(model=None, *, size=800, pixels_per_unit=10.0, perspective=0.0, rotation=0.0, blur=0.0, noise=0.0, gradient=0.0, glare=0.0, jpeg_quality=None, samples=8, rng=None)[source]¶

Render one synthetic photograph of model with exact ground-truth coverage.

Degradations are applied in capture order – geometry, then optics (blur, glare), then illumination, then sensor noise, then compression – because any other order models a camera that does not exist. perspective jitters the four corners by that fraction of the mark’s span; rotation is in degrees; gradient is the fractional illumination falloff across the frame.

Return type:

inkmap.synth.render_patch(*, size=(600, 800), patch_bgr=(60.0, 110.0, 200.0), paper_bgr=(232.0, 236.0, 238.0), background_bgr=(70.0, 80.0, 95.0), patch_size=(40.0, 24.0), paper_size=(100.0, 32.0), pixels_per_unit=6.0, centre=None, perspective=0.0, rotation=0.0, blur=0.0, noise=0.0, gradient=0.0, background_texture=0.0, supersample=8, rng=None)[source]¶

Render a uniform colour patch centred on a paper strip, over a background.

Model space is the strip’s own, in units: a paper_size strip with a patch_size patch at its centre, mapped into the frame at pixels_per_unit around centre (the frame’s centre by default), then rotated by rotation degrees and its corners jittered by perspective of the strip’s span. Edges are anti-aliased by drawing at supersample times the resolution and area-averaging, so the true patch quad is exact to that sampling. background_texture adds low-frequency blotches of that many grey levels to the background (a table, a cloth). Degradations then apply per channel, as in degrade().

Return type:

PatchScene
