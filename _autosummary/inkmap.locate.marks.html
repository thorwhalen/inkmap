inkmap.locate.marks¶

Find square registration marks: solid squares and nested squares, sub-pixel.

The other locators here find marks that have a decoder. Printed targets also carry plain registration marks – a solid square, or a bullseye of nested squares at each corner of a sheet, as on OMR forms – and pose estimation needs their centres to a small fraction of a pixel. find_square_marks() finds them:

from inkmap.locate import BULLSEYE_RINGS, find_square_marks

marks = find_square_marks(gray)
bullseyes = [m for m in marks if m.ring_error(BULLSEYE_RINGS) < 0.06]
bullseyes[0].centre, bullseyes[0].sigma_px

It knows what a square mark looks like, not which mark is which on a particular layout: naming them, by ring ratio and by where they sit relative to each other, is the caller’s.

A nested mark is a foreground square holding a background square holding a foreground square. A bullseye (rings at 0.64 and 0.32 of the side) and a QR symbol’s finder pattern (7:5:3 modules) are both of this kind, and they are told apart by their ring ratios, which perspective barely changes. A solid mark is a foreground square with nothing inside it, and reports no rings.

Centres are projective. The centre of a square seen in perspective is where its image’s diagonals cross, not the centroid of its pixels, which is pulled towards the near side. Each side is fitted as a line to its contour points, the corners are the lines’ intersections, and the centre is where the diagonals cross. The sides are placed on the grey image’s mid-level edge, not on the binarised outline, so the threshold does not move them: one that grew the foreground would move all four sides out alike, which leaves the diagonals’ crossing in place for a square seen head-on but not for one in strong perspective. The mid-level edge is exact for a linear sensor response; a gamma-encoded image moves a blurred edge a little (a worst centre error of 0.16 px was measured at strong perspective and blur 2 px with gamma 1/2.2).

Uncertainty. Each mark carries sigma_px, an estimate of how far its centre is from the truth: the scatter of the side fits over the square root of the points per side, combined, for a nested mark, with the disagreement between its squares’ centres (its centre is their side-weighted mean, since perspective maps them to one point), and for a solid mark, which has nothing to disagree with, with a fixed floor for the biases the fit cannot see. Both are calibrated on synthetic sheets so that the median centre error is about sigma_px; the tail is heavier than a Gaussian’s (one mark in twenty is off by 3-6 sigma), so weigh marks by it, but do not gate on it. A mark cut by the frame’s edge is dropped, never measured.

Limits. Each ring must be several blur widths across to survive binarisation: at blur 1.5 px a bullseye needs roughly 40 px or more, a solid square 10 px. Marks over max_side_share of the frame (10% by default) are not looked for; raising it widens the local-mean window with it, which tolerates uneven lighting less well. A disc under about 10 px radius fits four lines as well as a square of its size does, so it cannot be told from one. Anything square is a candidate, so a QR symbol’s clusters of data modules come back as small solid marks.

class inkmap.locate.marks.SquareMark(centre, corners, side_px, rings=(), sigma_px=nan, centres=())[source]¶

Bases: object

One square mark found in an image. Coordinates are pixels, (x, y).

corners are the outer square’s, clockwise as seen on screen, starting from the one nearest the image’s top-left. A square has no intrinsic first corner, so that order is a convention, not an identification. rings is the side of each nested square as a fraction of the outer one, outermost first (empty for a solid mark). centres are the outer square’s centre followed by each nested square’s, each where that square’s diagonals cross; centre is their mean weighted by side, which for a solid mark is the outer square’s own.

inkmap.locate.marks.find_square_marks(gray, *, min_side_px=8.0, max_side_share=0.1, polarity=Polarity.FOREGROUND_IS_DARK, dark_share=0.72, min_contrast=6.0, local_window_share=0.12)[source]¶

Every solid or nested square mark in gray (2-D, 0-255 scale), largest first.

A mark’s outer square must be at least min_side_px on a side and at most max_side_share of the frame’s shorter side. Nested marks report their ring ratios; solid marks report none. polarity says whether the marks read darker than the substrate (the usual case) or brighter.

The foreground is binarised relative to the local mean brightness: a pixel is foreground when it is darker than dark_share of the mean over a window local_window_share of the frame’s shorter side, and darker by at least min_contrast grey levels. The window is widened to stay wider than the largest mark looked for, since a mark wider than it is hollowed by its own ink.

Anything square is a candidate: a QR symbol’s clusters of data modules and some printed glyphs come back as solid marks too. Telling the registration marks from them, by size, ring ratio and layout, is the caller’s.
