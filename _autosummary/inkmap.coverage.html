inkmap.coverage

inkmap.coverage¶

Per-pixel foreground coverage: the 0..1 value, and which one you want.

Three different 0..1 quantities get conflated by “how foreground is this pixel”. They answer different questions, and mixing them produces numbers that mean nothing:

Coverage (coverage_map(), observed_coverage()) is physical: the fraction of the pixel’s area lying under foreground. ISO 12647-2:2013 3.1 calls this area coverage. A pixel straddling a mark’s edge genuinely is 0.37 foreground. The same quantity is the matting alpha (identical, for opaque marks), the remote-sensing fractional abundance of an endmember in a mixed pixel, and the medical-imaging partial volume fraction.

Confidence (confidence()) is epistemic: how sure we are of the label. This is what the user’s “probability that the pixel is foreground” means when the underlying reality is genuinely binary. A pixel deep inside a mark has coverage 1.0 and confidence 1.0; one in a blown-out highlight may have modelled coverage 1.0 and confidence near 0.

Weight (measurement_weight()) is operational: how safe the pixel is to measure, given that blur spreads signal across boundaries. It is the only one that should gate a physical measurement.

Two routes to coverage¶

Modelled (coverage_map()): the shape is known, so back-project a supersampled grid of each pixel through the transform and ask the model. Exact up to the supersampling rate, immune to lighting, and the right default. It answers “where should the foreground be”.

Observed (observed_coverage()): normalise the photograph between a background endpoint and a solid-foreground endpoint. This is the Murray-Davies inversion, and it answers “where is the foreground actually” – so it is the one that can see a printing defect. It returns an apparent (optical) coverage, inflated relative to geometric coverage by lateral light scattering in the substrate (the Yule-Nielsen effect), which is why the two routes disagree.

inkmap.coverage.confidence(gray, placement, coverage, *, polarity=Polarity.FOREGROUND_IS_DARK)[source]¶

How much to trust the coverage value at each pixel, in [0, 1].

Distinct from coverage. Three things erode it, combined multiplicatively:

  • Model/image disagreement – where the observed value contradicts the modelled coverage, one of them is wrong. This is what catches foreground that failed to print, a torn label, or a mis-fitted transform.

  • Clipping – saturated or crushed pixels carry no photometric information.

  • Edge proximity – mixed pixels are inherently less certain, and their value depends on sub-pixel geometry known only to ~0.5 px.

Return type:

ndarray

inkmap.coverage.coverage_map(model, transform, image_shape, *, samples=8)[source]¶

Area fraction of each pixel covered by the model’s foreground, in [0, 1].

Each pixel is probed at samples**2 sub-positions on a regular grid at sub-pixel centres; each probe is back-projected into model coordinates and passed to Model.occupancy(). The mean is the covered area fraction – ordinary supersampled area coverage, the same quantity a renderer’s anti-aliasing computes.

Values are quantised to 1/samples**2, so samples sets the accuracy floor. Only the model’s bounding box is evaluated, so a mark occupying 2% of a 12 MP photo costs 2% of the work.

Return type:

ndarray

inkmap.coverage.estimate_blur_sigma(gray, placement)[source]¶

Estimate imaging blur sigma, in pixels, from the mark’s own edges.

Sizing the measurement margin needs this. Estimated by finding the sigma whose blurred ideal coverage best matches the photograph.

Returns nan when the minimum lands at the top of the search range: that is the search running out of room, not a measurement – the modelled shape and the photograph do not correspond – and reporting the boundary value would silently size the measurement margin from noise.

Return type:

float

inkmap.coverage.measurement_weight(coverage, *, blur_sigma=None, radius=None, inside=None, kind='foreground', sigmas=2.0)[source]¶

Blur-aware weight in [0, 1]: how safe each pixel is to measure.

Coverage says a pixel lies entirely under foreground. That is necessary but not sufficient: the point spread function leaks neighbouring signal into interior pixels, and that contamination has a different footprint from partial area coverage – so no coverage-based weighting removes it. The fix is spatial: require a pixel’s whole neighbourhood to be foreground, out to the radius the blur reaches.

Implemented as grayscale morphological erosion of the coverage map, which is exactly “the minimum coverage within radius r” – continuous rather than a hard mask, so a pixel one step from an edge is down-weighted rather than trusted or discarded.

Give either radius (pixels, wins if both are given) or blur_sigma, from which the radius is sigmas * blur_sigma.

Return type:

ndarray

inkmap.coverage.observed_coverage(gray, placement, *, polarity=Polarity.FOREGROUND_IS_DARK, yule_nielsen_n=1.0, percentile=5.0)[source]¶

Apparent coverage read from the photograph itself (Murray-Davies).

Endmember values are estimated from the mark’s own pixels – its background region gives the substrate endpoint, its foreground core the solid endpoint – so no external calibration target is needed. Percentiles rather than extremes, so a specular highlight cannot define an endpoint.

yule_nielsen_n > 1 compensates for lateral light scattering in the substrate, which otherwise inflates apparent coverage above geometric coverage. n = 1 is plain Murray-Davies; the right value is substrate- and wavelength-specific and should be fitted, not guessed.

The result is apparent, not geometric, coverage. Do not silently substitute it for coverage_map().

Return type:

ndarray
